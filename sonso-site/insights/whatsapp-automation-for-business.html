WhatsApp automation for business: what to automate first

Your customers already reply on WhatsApp faster than on email. The question is which messages should go out without anyone typing them.

SoNSo · 6 min read

{% include article-banner.html %}

For many businesses, WhatsApp is where customers actually answer. But when it runs from one person's phone, it has the same problems as a shared inbox: messages get missed, nobody else can see the conversation, and follow-up depends on someone remembering.

Automation fixes that, as long as you automate the right messages first.

Personal app, Business app or Business Platform?

There are three ways a business uses WhatsApp, and only one of them supports real automation:

  • The personal app: fine for a sole trader, but not built for a team.
  • The WhatsApp Business app: adds a business profile, quick replies and simple away messages. Still one phone, still manual.
  • The WhatsApp Business Platform (the API): connects WhatsApp to your CRM, website and other systems, so messages can be sent automatically and several people can work from the same number.

Automated messaging runs on the Business Platform, from your verified business number.

Two rules to know before you start

Customers must opt in. People need to have agreed to hear from you on WhatsApp, for example by ticking a box on your form or messaging you first.

Templates outside the 24-hour window. When a customer messages you, you can reply freely for 24 hours. To start a conversation, or to message after that window, you use a pre-approved message template. Plan your key messages as templates from the start.

What to automate first

1. The instant reply to a new lead

When someone fills in your website form or an ad lead form, send a WhatsApp message within a minute: thank them, confirm what they asked about, and tell them what happens next. Leads contacted quickly are far more likely to talk to you than leads contacted the next day.

2. Booking confirmations and reminders

Confirm every appointment, consultation or booking, and send a reminder before it. This is one of the simplest ways to reduce no-shows.

3. Follow-ups that stop when the customer replies

If a lead goes quiet, a short, polite follow-up after a set time keeps the conversation alive. The key is that the sequence stops the moment they reply, so no one gets a robotic message after they've already answered.

4. Updates customers would otherwise call about

Order dispatched, payment received, document ready. Every update you send automatically is a phone call your team doesn't have to take.

5. Feedback after the job is done

A short message asking how it went, sent at the right moment, gets far more responses than an email survey.

Connect it to your CRM

WhatsApp automation works best when it isn't a separate tool. When messages are triggered by your CRM, every lead has an owner, every conversation is visible to the team, and you can measure what matters: how fast you respond, and how many conversations turn into customers.

What not to automate

Don't automate the conversation that wins the deal. Automation should handle the routine messages so your team has more time for the ones that need a person.
