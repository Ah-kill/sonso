Your ads report clicks. Your business runs on customers.

Most ad reports end at the moment someone fills in a form. Everything you care about happens after that.

SoNSo · 6 min read

Open a typical monthly ad report and you'll see impressions, clicks, click-through rate and cost per lead. Those numbers are real, but they answer the platform's question: did people respond to the ad? They don't answer yours: did the ad bring in customers, and was it worth the money?

Why the report stops at the click

Ad platforms only see what happens on their side. Once a lead leaves Meta or Google, it moves into an inbox, a spreadsheet or a CRM, and then into a phone call or a meeting. Unless someone connects those systems, the platform never learns which leads turned into sales.

The result is that campaigns get optimised for whatever the platform can see. A campaign that produces lots of cheap, unqualified leads looks like a winner. A campaign that produces fewer, better leads looks expensive.

Three things that fix it

1. Track the conversion, not just the click

Set up conversion tracking properly on every platform you use: GA4, the Meta Pixel with the Conversions API, and Google Ads conversions. Track phone calls and WhatsApp clicks too, because many buyers never fill in a form.

2. Carry the source into your CRM

Every lead should arrive in your CRM with the campaign that produced it attached. That usually means consistent campaign tags on every ad, and a website form or lead form that passes them through. Without this, nobody can trace a sale back to its ad.

3. Report on qualified leads and revenue

Once the source travels with the lead, you can report on what matters: cost per qualified lead, and revenue by campaign. Better still, feed that information back to the ad platforms so they optimise toward leads that become customers.

What changes when you do this

  • You stop scaling campaigns that only produce cheap, unqualified leads.
  • Your sales team's feedback on lead quality shows up in the numbers.
  • Budget decisions are made on revenue, not on click-through rate.

A quick self-check

Ask yourself: can you name the campaign that produced your best customer last month? If the honest answer is "no", the problem isn't your ads. It's the gap between your ads, your website and your CRM.
