Power BI or Looker Studio: which dashboard tool fits your business?

Both can give management a clear daily view of the business. The right choice depends on where your data lives and who needs to see it.

SoNSo · 6 min read

{% include article-banner.html %}

Microsoft Power BI and Google Looker Studio are two of the most common tools for building business dashboards. They overlap a lot, but they come from different worlds, and that shapes which one suits you.

The short answer

  • Lean towards Looker Studio if your business runs on Google: Google Sheets, Google Analytics, Google Ads, Google Workspace. It's free to use and quick to start.
  • Lean towards Power BI if your business runs on Microsoft and Excel, your data is complex or comes from many systems, or you need heavier modelling and calculations.

Where your data lives

Looker Studio connects natively to Google's own products, including Sheets, GA4, Google Ads and BigQuery. Other sources usually go through partner connectors, some of which are paid, or through a Google Sheet or database in between.

Power BI has a very wide range of built-in connectors, including Excel, SQL databases, SharePoint and many business applications, and it's strong at combining several sources into one data model.

Modelling and calculations

If your reports need things like target-versus-actual by rep and region, year-on-year comparisons and calculated margins, Power BI's data modelling is more powerful. Looker Studio handles straightforward reporting well, but complex logic usually needs to be prepared before the data reaches it.

Sharing and cost

Looker Studio reports are free to create and share, much like sharing a Google Doc.

Power BI Desktop, used to build reports, is free. Sharing reports with colleagues through the Power BI service generally requires paid licences, so the cost grows with the number of people who need to view them. Check Microsoft's current licensing for your situation.

Ease of use for management

Both produce dashboards that work in a browser and on a phone. What matters more than the tool is the design: a handful of numbers that answer management's questions at a glance, refreshed automatically, with one agreed definition for each metric.

A simple way to decide

  1. List the systems your numbers come from.
  2. List who needs to see the dashboard, and how often.
  3. List the three or four decisions the dashboard must support.

If the answers point to Google tools and simple reporting, start with Looker Studio. If they point to Excel-heavy finance, many data sources or complex calculations, Power BI is usually the better long-term fit.

Either way, the data comes first

Neither tool can fix messy data. Most dashboard projects spend their real effort on connecting systems, cleaning records and agreeing definitions. Get that right and either tool will serve you well.
