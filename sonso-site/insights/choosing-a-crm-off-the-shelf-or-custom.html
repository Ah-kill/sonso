Choosing a CRM for a growing sales team: off-the-shelf or custom?

The best CRM is the one your team actually uses every day. That depends less on features and more on how you sell.

SoNSo · 6 min read

{% include article-banner.html %}

Most CRM projects don't fail because the software is bad. They fail because the CRM doesn't match how the team sells, so people go back to spreadsheets and the CRM becomes a place where data goes to be forgotten.

Before you compare products, get clear on your sales process.

Start with how you sell

Answer these questions first. They decide most of the choice for you:

  • Where do leads come from? Website forms, ads, phone calls, walk-ins, distributors, referrals.
  • Who works them? One salesperson, a team, or a telecalling floor making dozens of calls a day.
  • What are the stages? From first contact to paid order, written down as your team actually works.
  • What does management need to see? Pipeline value, calls per rep, conversion by source, revenue against target.
  • What must it connect to? Website, WhatsApp, calling system, accounting, dashboards.

When an off-the-shelf CRM is right

Established CRMs such as Zoho, HubSpot or Salesforce cover the common sales process well: leads, deals, pipelines, tasks, email and reporting. They're the right choice when:

  • Your process looks broadly like a standard lead-to-deal pipeline.
  • You want to start quickly and grow into more features.
  • The integrations you need already exist.

The work is in setting it up properly: stages that match your process, automatic lead capture, owner assignment and reminders. An off-the-shelf CRM installed with default settings is just a more expensive spreadsheet.

When a custom CRM makes sense

Some sales processes don't fit a standard pipeline. A manufacturer selling to distributors, wholesalers and retailers by phone, for example, cares about call outcomes, repeat orders, schemes and territory targets more than about "deals". A custom CRM is worth considering when:

  • Your team would need so many workarounds that they'd stop using a standard CRM.
  • The thing you track isn't a deal: orders, calls, visits, dealers or bookings.
  • You need the CRM to drive specific reports, such as daily MIS against revenue targets.

A custom build takes longer and needs maintaining, so it should solve a problem a standard CRM genuinely can't.

Whatever you choose, get these right

  1. Automatic lead capture. Nobody should type a lead in by hand if it arrived through a form or an ad.
  2. Every lead has an owner and a next step. Otherwise follow-up still depends on memory.
  3. The source travels with the lead. So you can tell which campaigns bring customers, not just enquiries.
  4. Clean migration. Bring your spreadsheet history across, so the team trusts the new system from day one.
  5. Training. A short, practical handover does more for adoption than any feature.

Measure adoption, not installation

The real test comes a month after launch. Are new leads appearing automatically? Is every lead assigned? Is management reading its reports from the CRM rather than asking for a spreadsheet? If yes, the CRM is working.
